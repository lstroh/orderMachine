# Bin Sticker Idea Board — Solutions & Methodology Reference

*Companion to `bin_sticker_idea_gallery.html`. Use this to re-examine any of the 52 catalogued ideas for a way to produce them without the Cricut, or to understand why a given verdict was reached. IDs match the gallery exactly (P01–P48, with P09a–d and P30a–b as grouped duplicates).*

---

## 1. The core principle (read this first)

The single biggest lesson from this review: **"needs fine detail or multiple colours" is not the same problem as "needs Cricut."**

Fine detail and colour are only hard to produce with **cut vinyl** — a single colour of adhesive vinyl, physically cut along every outline, then hand-weeded (the excess peeled away). Thin strokes, small text, fur, whiskers, wings — all genuinely hard to weed cleanly, and that's the source of most "avoid thin/delicate designs" advice found online for vinyl cutting.

**None of that applies to this business's actual production method: print (inkjet) → laminate → guillotine-trim a rectangle.** An inkjet printer doesn't care whether it's printing a straight line or a raccoon's individual whiskers — both cost the same ink and the same few seconds. The only things that genuinely still require the Cricut are:

1. **The final sticker needs to be a shape other than a rectangle, with no card/background** (a true die-cut oval, house outline, hexagon, wreath ring) — cutting the *shape itself* is unavoidable.
2. **The material itself must be cut, not printed** (reflective/holographic vinyl — see §4).

Everything else — however detailed, however many colours, however "delicate" it would be as cut vinyl — is achievable today by printing the design as ink on the standard rectangle card and guillotine-trimming it, exactly like the current 10 designs.

**This was not applied consistently from the start.** Early in the review, ~35 of the 52 entries were marked "needs Cricut" primarily because of fine detail or full colour, using cut-vinyl reasoning that doesn't apply to this production method. This was caught and corrected retroactively once P44 made the pattern obvious (see §6). If re-examining older notes or memory of this review, **trust the "cutting" field in the gallery as it stands now, not the original first-pass reasoning for any given entry.**

## 2. Technique catalogue — ways to get a "needs Cricut" idea to "doable now"

*The gallery has a live **Solutions** dropdown filter matching the codes below (WC/CV/PTC/Cricut/NA) — filter directly instead of cross-referencing by hand.*

### A. Print the decorative shape as ink instead of cutting it
The border, frame, wreath, circle, or outline is redrawn as printed artwork *inside* a plain rectangle card, rather than physically cut to that outline. The rectangle is guillotine-trimmed as normal; the decorative shape is just ink.

**Applies to:** P01 (fluted border), P02 (house+floral silhouette), P04 (oval crest), P06 (floral wreath), P10 (house-outline frame), P12 (scrollwork rectangle), P17 (circle outline), P20 (house-outline frame), P26 (circle + flourish), P28 (arrow/fletching wreath), P30a/P30b (floral wreath, number-only)

### B. Swap an irregular card shape for a plain rectangle
Where the *background card itself* is a non-rectangular shape (oval, etc.) but the illustration on it doesn't depend on that shape, swap it for a rectangle. Nothing about the artwork is lost.

**Applies to:** P13 (grumpy cat oval → rectangle), P22 (resize the cats to sit within the card instead of overlapping the top edge)

### C. Fine detail / full colour, printed rather than cut
Illustrations with real complexity (fur, wings, spot patterns, multi-colour scenes) are fully producible once printed on a card — no simplification needed at all.

**Applies to:** P03, P05, P14, P16, P31, P32, P33, P36, P37, P38, P39, P40, P42, P43, P45, P47, P48 (and more — see the gallery's `doable_now` list)

### D. Single small Print-Then-Cut shape (different from the main product)
Your Equipment Guide rules out Cricut Print-Then-Cut for the main 4-up 100×140mm sheet — the combined layout exceeds the registration-mark size limit. But **one small individual shaped sticker is well within that limit.** Print-Then-Cut is genuinely viable as a *separate, standalone shaped product*, just not for the main bin sticker format.

**Applies to:** P19 (filled colour house shape), P35 (comedic cat holding a badge)

### E. White ink does not exist on your printer
Any design that is **white ink applied direct to a coloured bin** (no card background) cannot be printed as-is: your Epson EcoTank has no white ink, and no consumer inkjet does. Printing on clear vinyl doesn't fix this — the "white" areas just become "no ink," i.e. transparent, so the design disappears instead of showing white.

**Fix options:** (1) redesign in a printable dark colour instead of white, or (2) accept as a genuine Tier 3 future item — true white-on-clear printing is a standard UV DTF printer feature (eufyMake E1, already in the Growth Plan).

**Applies to:** P01 specifically identified this. The same logic applies to *any* pin showing white vinyl direct on a bin if you ever want to reproduce the white-on-colour look exactly rather than adapting it onto a white card.

### F. Clear vinyl + a printable colour, as an alternative to white ink
For designs whose ONLY blocker is "white ink direct on a coloured bin" (Technique E), rather than accepting these as future/Tier-3-only, print the design in a non-white colour on **clear** vinyl instead. This can't fully replicate a true white-on-bin look (see the opacity caveat below), but gets close enough to be usable now for most designs, and preserves the "no visible card" look many of the original pins have — closer to the original than the white-card workaround (Techniques A–C), which always shows a visible rectangle.

**Important caveat, not yet tested:** inkjet ink printed on clear vinyl is not fully opaque (some clear inkjet film specs show as little as ~9% opacity) — the bin colour underneath shows through and can mute the printed colour, especially on black. This technique needs the physical test described in `Bin-Sticker-Material-Test-Plan.md`, Addendum 2, before relying on it for real orders.

**5 candidate colours** (avoiding green/brown, since those would blend into 2 of the 3 most common bin colours):

| # | Colour | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Golden Yellow | `#F2B705` |
| 2 | Warm Cream/Ivory | `#F5E8C8` |
| 3 | Burnt Amber/Orange | `#D9782E` |
| 4 | Powder Sky Blue | `#8FB8DE` |
| 5 | Dusty Rose/Coral | `#E08A73` |

**When to use F vs. A–C:** Technique F only makes sense for designs that were originally white/single-colour ink applied *direct to the bin with no card* — for anything already shown on its own white/light card in the original pin (e.g. P13, P21, P24, P25, P27, P44, P47), there's no white-ink problem to solve, so the standard white-card method (A–C) already reproduces it exactly with no adaptation needed.

## 4. Material-only blockers — not a shape problem, not solvable by printing

Reflective and holographic adhesive vinyl is a physically different material from your printable inkjet stock. It isn't something you print on — it's a pre-made reflective sheet that has to be *cut* to shape regardless of how simple or complex the design is. Printing doesn't apply here at all; this is a genuinely separate material line that would need sourcing and costing from scratch.

**Applies to:** P29, P41 (two independent sightings — worth treating as a real future material category, tied to the reflective/night-glow differentiator already flagged in the original Competitor Research file)

## 6. How the correction happened (P44)

P44 (EDSG's "Design 9," a fluted-border number-only sticker, "#1 Best Seller in Signs & Plaques") looked structurally identical to P01 from the very first batch — same scalloped border concept — but P44 was clearly printed on a white card rather than applied direct-to-bin. That removed P01's white-ink blocker entirely and made obvious the pattern described in §1: most "needs Cricut" verdicts up to that point were really "needs Cricut *if cut from vinyl*," not "needs Cricut *no matter what*."

After that, every prior entry was re-passed against the corrected principle. The result: **the doable-now count went from 4 of 48 to 46 of 52.**

## 8. Open questions this review surfaced (not yet decided)

- **Number-only sizing:** several validated number-only ideas (P18, P30a/b, P44, P46) don't need the full 140mm card height built for number+street. Worth deciding whether to define a dedicated smaller "number only" size/price tier, or keep using the existing card with the street line left blank.
- **Caddy sticker sizing:** P08, P16, P20, and P23 all independently point at a matching bin+caddy bundle. A caddy needs its own measured size and its own cost-sheet line — not just a shrunk version of the 100×140mm spec.
- **Colour range:** P38 offers 22 colours; the current palette has 6. Not necessarily worth matching, but worth having considered deliberately rather than by default.

## 9. Checklist for reviewing any future pinned idea

Use this order of questions when a new image comes in:

1. **Is it actually a sticker?** Rule out wall plaques, solar signs, or other hardware first (§5).
2. **Does the final product need to be a non-rectangular shape, with no card background at all?** If yes → genuine Cricut/Print-Then-Cut requirement (§3). If no → keep going.
3. **Is it printed on white ink direct to a coloured surface?** If yes → check the white-ink blocker (§2E) before assuming it's simple.
4. **Is the material itself special (reflective, holographic, glow-in-the-dark)?** If yes → material-only blocker, not a shape one (§4).
5. **Otherwise** — however detailed, however many colours — it's doable now: print it as ink on the standard rectangle card, guillotine-trim as usual.
