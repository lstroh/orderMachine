## 1. On-platform tools available

### Promoted Listings — General (formerly "Standard")
- **Cost-per-sale.** You set an ad rate (% of sale price); eBay charges it *only* if the item sells via a promoted click, within the attribution window.
- No sale, no charge — no risk of "wasted" spend on views or clicks that don't convert.
- This is the right tool for a small/single-product-line launch. **Recommended default for this business.**

### Promoted Listings — Priority (formerly "Advanced")
- **Cost-per-click (CPC).** You bid on keywords and set a daily budget; you pay per click *regardless of whether it sells*.
- Fixed-price listings only. Requires active bid/budget management.
- Since January 2026, only Priority campaigns can reach eBay's top ad slot — General campaigns lost access to that placement.
- **Not recommended at launch.** Built for sellers with larger catalogues and dedicated ad management time. Revisit only once volume and margins justify active bid management.

### Seller coupons / Markdown Manager (Sale Events)
- **Requires an active eBay Store subscription** (from ~£4.95/month). Not available to a non-Store seller.
- Two flavours: store-wide markdown sales, and coded/codeless coupons.
- **Not available at launch** unless a Store subscription is added — currently not budgeted anywhere in the project files.

## 2. What to actually spend — benchmarks

**Important distinction: eBay's own "suggested" ad rate is not a neutral recommendation.** It's optimised for eBay's revenue, not seller margin, and independent sources consistently flag it as inflated.

| Source | Typical guidance |
|---|---|
| eBay's own suggested rate | Often 8-15%, sometimes higher |
| Independent/practitioner guidance | 2-4% as a working range; "should never exceed 3-4% of total revenue" |
| Seller community reports | Wide range (2-30%), generally lower when sellers deliberately undercut eBay's suggestion |

**Practical rule for this business: start at 2-4%, not eBay's suggested rate.** Treat eBay's number as a ceiling to negotiate down from, not a target.

**Fix free things first.** Promoted Listings only amplifies a listing that already converts — sharp photos, complete item specifics, and a clear title matter more than the ad rate itself, especially at low volume where every visible defect is disproportionately costly.

## 3. How ad spend stacks with other eBay fees

Promoted Listings fees are **additional to**, not included in, standard selling fees. Total platform cost = Final Value Fee + per-order fee + regulatory fee + ad spend.

| Fee | Rate (UK business seller, checked Aug 2026) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Final Value Fee | 12.8% of item price + postage (most categories) | Category-dependent, 6.9-14.9% range |
| Per-order fee | £0.30 (orders under £10) / £0.40 (orders ≥£10) | Changed Feb 2026 — verify this hasn't moved again |
| Regulatory Operating Fee | ~0.35-0.43% of item price + postage | Introduced April 2024, applies regardless of ad usage |
| Promoted Listings (General) | Your chosen ad rate, e.g. 2-4% | Only charged on a sale via a promoted click |

**At a low unit price, the flat 30p/40p per-order fee eats a disproportionate share** — worth remembering when comparing total fee % across different price points, and a real argument for bundling (spreads the flat fee across more revenue per order).

## 4. Recommended launch approach (eBay-first)

1. **Turn on Promoted Listings (General) from day one**, on all listings, starting at **2-4%**. No reason to hold back a subset the way you would with CPC, since there's no cost unless it sells.
2. **Do not enable Priority/CPC campaigns** at launch — save for once volume and margin are established.
3. **Do not add an eBay Store subscription / coupons** at launch — not justified at current volume; revisit once order volume is consistently higher.
4. **No separate off-platform ad budget needed at launch** — off-eBay marketing (social, own-site ads) is out of scope while eBay-first sequencing is in effect.
5. **Review after ~30 days of real sales data.** Adjust the ad rate up or down based on actual conversion, not further guessing. Pause promotion on anything with high impressions and no sales after ~14 days.
6. **Target listings that need the help** once there's more than one SKU — items sitting 30+ days with low views and margin to spare are the best candidates for promotion, not best-sellers that would have sold anyway.
