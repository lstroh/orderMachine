# Listing Assets Reference — Water Bottle / Bag / Laptop Decals

*Four presentation/marketing insights pulled from the decal idea board (`decal_idea_gallery.html`), tagged there as "Listing/marketing insight" entries. None of these are sticker designs — they're reusable patterns for how to *present* whatever designs you end up selling. Source entries: D18, D28, D29, D30, D36, D47.*

---

> **⚠️ eBay Picture Policy check (1 Oct 2026, read directly from ebay.co.uk/help/policies/picture-policy, id=4370):** eBay UK listing photos may NOT contain added text, artwork or marketing material, added borders, badges, logos, or watermarks of any type, and may not be placeholder images used to convey messages. Every element below that is overlaid on a photo (header, subheading, icon bullets, trust badges, "Decal Only" callout) is **not permitted on eBay listing photos**. Enforcement is patchy and many competitors break it, but the penalty can be removal, warning, restriction or suspension, which is a disproportionate risk for a new account. On eBay, put this information in the title, item specifics and description instead, and show size physically (ruler/coin/hand in frame). These patterns may still be usable on Etsy or the own website, whose rules have not been checked yet.

## 1. Hero image structure (source: D28)

The main listing photo template — a header, a subheading, and four trust bullets, built around one product shot.

```
HEADER (3 lines, ~3 words total):
  [PRODUCT CATEGORY WORD] [accent-colour product noun] [PRODUCT CATEGORY WORD]
  e.g. "CUSTOM name STICKER"

SUBHEADING (1 line):
  "Perfect for [surface 1], [surface 2] & more!"
  — name 2 concrete surfaces the product actually fits

FEATURE BULLETS (4, icon + 2–4 word label each):
  [icon] MATERIAL/DURABILITY CLAIM      e.g. "WATERPROOF VINYL"
  [icon] QUALITY CLAIM                  e.g. "DURABLE & LONG LASTING"
  [icon] PERSONALISATION CLAIM          e.g. "PERSONALIZED JUST FOR YOU"
  [icon] USE-CASE CLAIM                 e.g. "PERFECT FOR EVERYDAY USE"

PRODUCT SHOT:
  — styled with 2–3 soft lifestyle props, not a plain white background
  — positioned beside the text block, not overlapping it
```

**Why it works:** answers "what is this / will it work for my item / can I trust it" in the order a browsing shopper actually asks, and the icon+label format survives being shrunk to a search-results thumbnail — a paragraph making the same claims wouldn't.

**Guardrail:** only make claims the Material Test Plan actually backs up for that specific material/finish. Standard laminated Premium Vinyl Permanent is a safe "waterproof" claim; holographic and metallic finishes currently read "water-resistant, indoor decor" on their own product listings — don't carry the same claim over without testing.

## 2. Trust badges (source: D36)

Small badge callouts — icon + short bold text, same visual convention as the hero-image bullets — placed as a secondary element on the listing photo (footer or corner), not the main hero block.

- **"Save when you buy multiples"** (tag icon) — bulk-discount signal. Pairs naturally with a bundle offer (see D11: same sticker design, resized and sold across a matching multi-item set).
- **"Free 1st Class Postage"** (delivery truck icon) — genuine Royal Mail terminology, matches your existing Shipping Plan directly. **Only use this if the shipping cost is actually built into your unit price** — don't advertise "free" postage you haven't accounted for.

## 3. "Decal Only" disclaimer (source: D47)

A single bold text callout across the top or corner of any photo that shows your decal applied to an item you don't sell yourself (a bottle, bag, or laptop that's the customer's own).

**Why it matters:** most of your product photography will necessarily show the decal on a blank you sourced for demo purposes, not something included in the sale. A plain "Decal Only" label heads off the obvious confusion — a customer assuming the whole bottle ships with the sticker — before it becomes a support message or a dispute.

Cheap to add, costs nothing, and should go on essentially every listing photo where the decal is shown applied to something.

## 4. Menu size as a headline feature (source: D18, D29, D30)

Real competitors don't bury their customisation range in fine print — they put the number in the actual marketing copy:

- D18: "TAREAS" font-pairing showcase names its exact fonts as a feature
- D29: a listing shows 18 real font examples across one image
- D30: a real seller's headline reads **"95 font options!"** — stated outright, not just implied

**Practical takeaway:** you don't need 95 fonts to start. The realistic target is the reference sheet already suggested from D29 (12–20 names across 4–5 pre-cleared Google Fonts families) and D39 (8–12 named colours from your actual stock, with a "subject to availability" disclaimer). But once that reference sheet exists, **state the count in your own listing title or header** — "20+ font styles," "12 colours" — rather than leaving "custom font" as a vague, uncounted option. The number itself is doing marketing work in every example this board found.

## Quick-reference checklist for a real listing photo

*Not for eBay photos — see the Picture Policy warning at the top. On eBay, only the last two lines apply, and only as description/title text, not on the image.*

- [ ] Header names the product in ~3 words
- [ ] Subheading names 2 concrete compatible surfaces
- [ ] 4 icon+label trust bullets, claims checked against the Material Test Plan for the actual material used
- [ ] Product shot styled with soft props, not bare white background
- [ ] "Decal Only" callout if the item shown isn't included in the sale
- [ ] Bulk-discount and/or postage badge, if genuinely offered and costed
- [ ] Font/colour count stated as a number somewhere in the copy, once the reference sheet exists
