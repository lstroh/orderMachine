**Note on the Explore 5:** it uses a new integrated blade/tool housing — the old Scoring Stylus from Explore 3/4 isn't compatible if you already own one. Doesn't affect Print-Then-Cut sticker work, only scoring (fold lines for cards etc).

**Updated role of the Explore 5 (July 2026):** Cricut Print Then Cut requires live margin around the design for its registration marks, and — per Cricut's own and third-party documentation — the maximum usable design area on A4 paper is meaningfully smaller than a full A4 sheet (roughly 7.2 x 10.62in / 182.9 x 269.7mm at best, sometimes as small as 6.75 x 9.25in / 171 x 235mm depending on Design Space version). Your planned bin sticker layout (4 x 100x140mm stickers, edge-to-edge on one A4 sheet) exceeds this in both dimensions, so it can't be cut as a single Print Then Cut pass. Since the bin sticker shape is a plain rounded rectangle (not a contour), it doesn't need Print Then Cut at all — see the Sep 2026 decision below. The Explore 5 remains genuinely useful for anything with an actual irregular/contoured shape (e.g. novelty or monogram water bottle & laptop decals — see Competitor Research), so it's still worth having in Tier 1, and, per the Sep 2026 decision below, also for bin stickers via cut-only.

**Decision (Sep 2026) — Cricut cut-only for every bin sticker size, including the 140×100mm baseline.** The Explore 5 kiss-cuts each rounded-rectangle outline (through vinyl and laminate, not the backing) at known coordinates, so it also rounds the corners. The cut sheet is then **trimmed into separate backed stickers** with the guillotine. This replaces the corner rounder punch and the Slice peel-nick from the July workflow. Test cuts on the real vinyl + laminate stock are needed to set blade depth and pressure, and to decide how the peel-start works once the backing is trimmed flush (see "Cutting & Finishing Tools" below).

**Explore 5's role, updated again (Sep 2026):** the machine is now purchased and its role has expanded beyond "irregular shapes only." Cut-only jobs (a fixed rectangle at known coordinates, no camera/registration marks — see the "cut only" explanation further down this file) sidestep the Print-Then-Cut registration-mark limit on *any* Cricut machine, not just a smaller one — so the already-owned Explore 5 can now also handle straight-line cutting for the Medium (202×140mm) and Large (280×200mm) size tiers being tested (see chat history, "Custom bin sticker sheet layout specifications" follow-up), not just Print-Then-Cut for irregular decals. This removes the need for the separate small machine described in "Kiss-Cut Upgrade Path — Option B" below, which is kept only as a fallback.

One real constraint surfaced by that testing, though: cut-only is limited by the **cutting mat's** maximum cut area, not the camera/registration limit. Confirmed specs for the Explore 5's mats: 12×12in mat → 11.5×11.5in (292.1×292.1mm) max cut area; 12×24in mat → 11.5×23.5in (292.1×596.9mm). Applied to the three sizes:
- **All three tiers fit a single 12×12 mat (Sep 2026 sizes):** Baseline (140×100mm cards, 4-up, cut area about 200×280mm), Medium (202×140mm, 2-up stacked, about 202×280mm) and Large (280×200mm, 1-up, about 200×280mm) — all under the 292.1mm limit.
- **The 12×24 mat is no longer needed for Medium.** The earlier 210×140mm size needed the full 297mm sheet height (about 5mm over the limit); the 202×140mm card stacks to 280mm.

Since cut-only has no camera to auto-align, the printed sheet needs to be squared up on the mat by hand (using the mat's own grid lines against the sheet's corner tick marks), rather than relying on Design Space's automatic registration — fine for a straight/rounded-rectangle cut, just a manual step that Print-Then-Cut doesn't require.

**12×24 mat: not needed (Sep 2026).** The Medium size was trimmed to 202×140mm, so both cards' outlines fit the 12×12 mat in one pass. Check which mats your Ultimate Bundle includes.

**Alternative source for vinyl + laminate together**: Cricut's own [Printable Waterproof Sticker Set – A4 (20 ct)](https://cricut.com/en/materials/material-type/printables-stickers/printable-waterproof-sticker-set-%E2%80%93-a4-20-ct/printable-waterproof-sticker-set-20ct.html?dwvar_printable-waterproof-sticker-set-20ct_color=Transparent) (white and transparent versions) bundles a matched vinyl sheet + laminate sheet per set, rated waterproof/UV-resistant up to 2 years. **Note on lamination:** for stickers (as opposed to laminating documents), you want **self-adhesive cold laminate**, not a hot thermal laminator machine — heat-activated pouches seal around the edges, which breaks a print-then-cut workflow. Most cheap "A4 laminators" sold on Amazon are thermal/hot — skip those for this purpose. The manual cold roll laminator above is the right type of machine for this job.

**File-compatibility note (why Joy Xtra is the easier one for me to help with):** cut files for a fixed rectangle like this are SVGs. Cricut Design Space imports SVG for free, no upgrade needed. Silhouette Studio's free Basic Edition **cannot** import SVG at all — it requires the Designer Edition upgrade (~£40–50 one-time) to open SVG files directly; the free tier's fallback is DXF import (needs a separate generator script) or PNG-plus-manual-tracing. If you go with Silhouette, ask for the DXF version of the cut-lines script instead of the SVG one below.

A ready-to-use SVG cut-lines file (matching `bin_sticker.py`'s exact 2×2 A4 grid, so cut lines land directly on the printed stickers) and the generator script that made it are available from this conversation — regenerate any time the card size changes.

Either machine needs a few test cuts on your actual Stickiply/vinyl stock to dial in blade depth/force for a clean kiss cut — settings aren't universal across sticker paper brands.

## TIER 3 — UV DTF Printer (best long-term durability, biggest investment)

**eufyMake E1** (entry-level desktop UV printer) — *re-checked, pricing unchanged*
- No lamination step needed — decals come out cured, waterproof, scratch-resistant, ready to apply
- $2,499 Basic bundle / $3,299 Deluxe bundle (~£1,950 / ~£2,580) — pricing has settled since Kickstarter, now a standard in-stock product
- Opens the door to printing on curved/hard surfaces later (mugs, bottles) if you expand beyond flat stickers
- [Buy from eufyMake UK](https://www.eufymake.com/uk/products/eufymake-e1)

**Worth watching, not yet buyable:** competitors (xTool UV printer, Longer ePrint) are announced for later-2026 release, expected at similar or higher price points — nothing cheaper to switch to yet, but worth a quick check back before committing to Tier 3 if you're not in a rush.

## Recommended path

1. **Start with Tier 1** (~£855–870 as bought/planned, including the cold roll laminator) — this is the genuine professional workflow (pigment ink + laminate) on current-generation equipment, not a compromise.
2. Sell for 3–6 months, work towards break-even (see Business Plan Section 9 — under revision; the earlier ~150–200 sales figure is superseded).
3. Reinvest profits into **Tier 3 (UV DTF)** if volume justifies it — better long-term quality than Tier 2, and opens up new product categories.
