*Source: D28 in the decal idea gallery (`decal_idea_gallery.html`), a "Custom Name Sticker" listing hero image. The sticker design in that source is a repeat of D01/D27 (white single-colour name) — the useful thing to keep from this source isn't the sticker, it's the page layout. This doc is a standalone reference for that layout so it doesn't get lost inside a product-idea entry.*

> **⚠️ eBay Picture Policy check (1 Oct 2026, read directly from ebay.co.uk/help/policies/picture-policy, id=4370):** eBay UK listing photos may NOT contain added text, artwork or marketing material, added borders, badges, logos, or watermarks of any type, and may not be placeholder images used to convey messages. Every element below that is overlaid on a photo (header, subheading, icon bullets, trust badges, "Decal Only" callout) is **not permitted on eBay listing photos**. Enforcement is patchy and many competitors break it, but the penalty can be removal, warning, restriction or suspension, which is a disproportionate risk for a new account. On eBay, put this information in the title, item specifics and description instead, and show size physically (ruler/coin/hand in frame). These patterns may still be usable on Etsy or the own website, whose rules have not been checked yet.

## What's in the image

A single "hero" listing image built from four stacked elements, left-to-right and top-to-bottom:

1. **Header** — "CUSTOM name STICKER," three lines: two in bold black caps ("CUSTOM" / "STICKER") bracketing one word in pink cursive ("name") with a small heart accent. The product category is stated in three words, no more.
2. **Subheading** — "PERFECT FOR WATER BOTTLES, TUMBLERS & MORE!" — one line, states exactly what surfaces the product fits and where its versatility ends ("& more" rather than an exhaustive list).
3. **Feature bullets** — four short lines, each with a small circular icon on the left and a 2–4 word label on the right:
   - Droplet icon → "WATERPROOF VINYL"
   - Sparkle icon → "DURABLE & LONG LASTING"
   - Heart icon → "PERSONALIZED JUST FOR YOU"
   - Checkmark icon → "PERFECT FOR EVERYDAY USE"
4. **Product shot** — a styled photo of the actual item (a purple water bottle with a white name decal applied), positioned to the right of the text, with soft desk props (flowers, notebooks, marble surface) establishing a lifestyle setting rather than a plain product-on-white shot.

## Why this structure works

**It answers the three questions a browsing shopper actually asks, in order, before they even read a description:**

- *What is this?* — the header, in three words.
- *Will it work for what I have?* — the subheading, naming the actual items (bottles, tumblers) rather than a vague "great gift" line.
- *Can I trust it?* — the four bullets, each pairing a scannable icon with a short claim (waterproof, durable, personalised, everyday-use). None of the four claims need a sentence to explain — a shopper skimming a search results grid can absorb all four in under two seconds.

**The icon + short-label pattern is doing real work, not just decoration.** A block of prose making the same four claims would take longer to read and wouldn't survive being shrunk to a thumbnail in search results. Four repeated small icons in a vertical list is a format that still reads clearly even when the image is small — which is exactly the size it'll usually be seen at first (a search grid, not full-screen).

**The product shot earns its place instead of being generic.** It's not a flat product-on-white cutout — it's staged with props (flowers, a notebook, marble) that suggest a lifestyle/gift context without adding any text of their own. The text block and the photo don't compete for attention because they're cleanly separated left/right rather than overlapping.

**Nothing in the layout is specific to name stickers.** The header names *your* product, the subheading names *your* compatible surfaces, the four bullets state *your* material claims, and the photo shows *your* actual item. The scaffolding — three-part header, one-line versatility statement, four icon-paired trust bullets, styled product shot — is reusable for any listing, not just this one design.

## Template to reuse

A fill-in-the-blank version of the same structure:

```
HEADER (3 lines, ~3 words total):
  [PRODUCT CATEGORY WORD] [accent-colour product noun] [PRODUCT CATEGORY WORD]
  e.g. CUSTOM name STICKER / PERSONALISED [word] DECAL

SUBHEADING (1 line):
  "Perfect for [surface 1], [surface 2] & more!"
  — name 2 concrete surfaces the product actually fits, don't just say "everything"

FEATURE BULLETS (4, icon + 2–4 word label each):
  [icon] MATERIAL/DURABILITY CLAIM      e.g. "WATERPROOF VINYL"
  [icon] QUALITY CLAIM                  e.g. "DURABLE & LONG LASTING"
  [icon] PERSONALISATION CLAIM          e.g. "PERSONALIZED JUST FOR YOU"
  [icon] USE-CASE CLAIM                 e.g. "PERFECT FOR EVERYDAY USE"
  — only make claims you can actually back up (see Material Test Plan before
    claiming "waterproof" on any material/finish combination not yet tested)

PRODUCT SHOT:
  — styled with 2–3 soft lifestyle props (not a plain white background)
  — positioned beside the text block, not overlapping it
  — shows the actual finished product on the actual intended item
```

**Before using this for a real listing:** only claim what's actually been verified. This image's "WATERPROOF VINYL" and "DURABLE & LONG LASTING" bullets are exactly the kind of claims that should be checked against the Material Test Plan for the specific material/finish combination being listed (e.g. standard Premium Vinyl Permanent is a safer waterproof claim than the holographic or metallic finishes, which current listings describe as "water-resistant" and "indoor decor" rather than outdoor-rated).
