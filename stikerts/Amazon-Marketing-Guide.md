# Amazon Marketing Guide — Bin Sticker Side Hustle

*Prepared August 2026 · UK Amazon · Companion to `eBay-Marketing-Guide.md` and `Etsy-Marketing-Guide.md`*

**Status: reference guide for later use, furthest out in the launch sequence.** Business Plan Section 12 (Growth Plan) treats Amazon as a later-phase channel with no committed timing — this guide is prepared ahead of that decision, not because it's imminent. Amazon's fee/ad structure is the most complex of the three and changes fee bands regularly, so treat everything below as directional and re-verify before acting.

---

## 1. The account-tier gate — different from eBay and Etsy

This is the biggest structural difference from the other two platforms: **on Amazon, advertising access is gated behind account type, not behind revenue or a separate subscription toggle.**

| | Individual plan | Professional plan |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | ~75p per item sold, no monthly fee | ~£25/month flat, no per-item fee |
| Sponsored Products (PPC ads) | **Not available** | Available |
| Buy Box eligibility | No | Yes |
| Crossover point | Cheaper below ~34 items/month | Cheaper at ~34+ items/month |

**Practical implication: no advertising at all is possible on Amazon without paying the ~£25/month Professional subscription first**, regardless of sales volume. This is a genuine fixed cost with no "only pay if it sells" version, unlike eBay's General campaigns or Etsy's Offsite Ads. Worth weighing against expected early Amazon volume before committing.

## 2. Selling fees (separate from advertising)

| Fee | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Referral fee | 8-15% of total sale price (category-dependent), most categories in this range | Minimum referral fee of £0.25-£1 per item applies regardless of price — matters at £2-4 unit prices |
| Amazon Handmade referral fee (if eligible) | 12.24% flat | Alternative to standard referral fee for genuinely handcrafted goods — see eligibility note below |
| Digital Services Tax pass-through | 2% | Added on top of referral fees for UK-established sellers, e.g. a 15% referral fee effectively costs 15.3% |
| Refund administration fee | Lesser of £5 or 20% of the referral fee, on returns | Relevant if return rates are non-trivial |
| FBA fees (if using Fulfilled by Amazon) | Storage + pick/pack + weight handling, varies | Not relevant if fulfilling orders yourself (FBM), which is the more natural fit at this scale |

**Amazon Handmade is worth checking eligibility for.** It's an artisan-only programme with free listing and a flat 12.24% referral fee — potentially cheaper than the standard category referral fee, and positioned closer to Etsy's model (curated handmade marketplace). Whether laminated vinyl bin stickers qualify as "handmade" under Amazon's specific criteria (genuinely hand-crafted or hand-assembled, not just personalised/printed) isn't confirmed — this needs checking directly against Amazon's Handmade application criteria before assuming it applies, since printed-and-cut vinyl sits in a grey area next to genuinely hand-made crafts.

## 3. Sponsored Products (PPC) — how it works and what it costs

- **Cost-per-click**, not cost-per-sale — closer to eBay's Priority/Advanced than to General/Standard. You pay per click regardless of conversion.
- Requires a Professional seller account (see Section 1).
- The standard efficiency metric is **ACoS (Advertising Cost of Sales)** = ad spend ÷ ad-attributed revenue. A 25% ACoS means 25p spent per £1 of ad-attributed sales.
- **Benchmarks vary a lot depending on source and goal:**
  - General "profitable" guidance: **15-25% ACoS** is considered low/profitable for an established product.
  - Platform-wide average across Sponsored Products users: reported around **34%**.
  - During a product launch phase, a **higher ACoS (30-40%+) is normal and often accepted deliberately** to build initial sales velocity and organic ranking — Amazon's own guidance is that chasing the lowest ACoS isn't always the right early move.
  - **The number only means something against your margin** — a 25% ACoS is fine at a 40% margin and a loss-maker at a 20% margin. Given this business's tight per-unit margins at £2-4 price points, ACoS needs checking against the *landed cost* figures in the costs workbook, not against generic benchmarks.
- **Starting budget suggestions in general guidance run £20-30/day equivalent** for a first automatic campaign to gather data — this is aimed at larger/higher-price sellers and is almost certainly oversized for a £2-4 sticker launch. Scale down proportionally, or treat this as a strong argument for holding off Amazon PPC until order volume and per-unit margin are firmly established elsewhere first.

## 4. How this compares to eBay and Etsy at a glance

| | eBay | Etsy | Amazon |
|---|---|---|---|
| Default ad model | Cost-per-sale (General) | Cost-per-sale (Offsite, conditional) + optional CPC (onsite) | Cost-per-click only (Sponsored Products) |
| Fixed cost to access ads | None | None | ~£25/month (Professional account required) |
| Can ads run with zero risk if nothing sells? | Yes (General) | Yes (Offsite, while below threshold) | **No** — CPC charges regardless of conversion |
| Recommended launch stance | On from day one, 2-4% | Off (Offsite) until forced; onsite optional, small budget | Hold off until Professional-tier volume and margin justify the fixed cost |

Amazon is structurally the **highest-commitment, highest-risk** of the three for a launch-stage seller — the only one where you can lose money on ads with zero sales, and the only one gating advertising behind a flat monthly fee. This reinforces the existing eBay-first, Amazon-last sequencing in the Business Plan rather than arguing against it.

## 5. Recommended approach once Amazon is next in the sequence

1. **Don't default to Professional/advertising on day one.** Consider listing as Individual first (or even holding Amazon listings back further) until there's a clear read on whether this channel adds incremental volume over eBay/Etsy.
2. **Check Amazon Handmade eligibility properly** before assuming the standard referral fee — could meaningfully change the fee comparison.
3. **If/when Sponsored Products is turned on, start small and expect a higher launch-phase ACoS** — budget scaled to a £2-4 item, not the £20-30/day figures aimed at higher-ticket sellers.
4. **Re-check margin math against the specific ACoS achieved**, not a generic benchmark, given how thin per-unit margins are at this price point.
5. **Treat this guide as provisional** — Amazon's fee schedule changes more frequently than eBay's or Etsy's, and this is the furthest-out channel, so a re-check immediately before launch is essential rather than optional.
