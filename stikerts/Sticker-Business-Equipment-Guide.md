**Note on the Explore 5:** it uses a new integrated blade/tool housing — the old Scoring Stylus from Explore 3/4 isn't compatible if you already own one. Doesn't affect Print-Then-Cut sticker work, only scoring (fold lines for cards etc).

**Updated role of the Explore 5 (July 2026):** Cricut Print Then Cut requires live margin around the design for its registration marks, and — per Cricut's own and third-party documentation — the maximum usable design area on A4 paper is meaningfully smaller than a full A4 sheet (roughly 7.2 x 10.62in / 182.9 x 269.7mm at best, sometimes as small as 6.75 x 9.25in / 171 x 235mm depending on Design Space version). Your planned bin sticker layout (4 x 100x140mm stickers, edge-to-edge on one A4 sheet) exceeds this in both dimensions, so it can't be cut as a single Print Then Cut pass. Since the bin sticker shape is a plain rounded rectangle (not a contour), it doesn't need Print Then Cut at all — see the **Cutting & Finishing Tools** and **Kiss-Cut Upgrade Path** sections below. The Explore 5 remains genuinely useful for anything with an actual irregular/contoured shape (e.g. novelty or monogram water bottle & laptop decals — see Competitor Research), so it's still worth having in Tier 1, just not as the bin sticker cutting method.

**Note on lamination:** for stickers (as opposed to laminating documents), you want **self-adhesive cold laminate**, not a hot thermal laminator machine — heat-activated pouches seal around the edges, which breaks a print-then-cut workflow. Most cheap "A4 laminators" sold on Amazon are thermal/hot — skip those for this purpose. The manual cold roll laminator above is the right type of machine for this job.

These three cheap tools together reproduce what a kiss-cutting machine would do, without needing Cricut Print Then Cut or its registration-mark size limit. **Option B — Small cutting machine (£130–180), for a machine-driven kiss cut**

Not Print Then Cut — since the sticker is a fixed 100×140mm rectangle rather than artwork needing tracing, this uses a **"cut only" job**: manually align the printed sheet on the mat (same corner tick marks already printed by `bin_sticker.py`), then cut a rectangle at known coordinates. This sidesteps Print Then Cut's registration-mark margin entirely, so a full A4 sheet's raw cutting area is usable — the constraint that ruled out the Explore 5 above doesn't apply here, because it's a different cutting method, not just a smaller machine.

**File-compatibility note (why Joy Xtra is the easier one for me to help with):** cut files for a fixed rectangle like this are SVGs. Cricut Design Space imports SVG for free, no upgrade needed. Silhouette Studio's free Basic Edition **cannot** import SVG at all — it requires the Designer Edition upgrade (~£40–50 one-time) to open SVG files directly; the free tier's fallback is DXF import (needs a separate generator script) or PNG-plus-manual-tracing. If you go with Silhouette, ask for the DXF version of the cut-lines script instead of the SVG one below.

A ready-to-use SVG cut-lines file (matching `bin_sticker.py`'s exact 2×2 A4 grid, so cut lines land directly on the printed stickers) and the generator script that made it are available from this conversation — regenerate any time the card size changes.

Either machine needs a few test cuts on your actual Stickiply/vinyl stock to dial in blade depth/force for a clean kiss cut — settings aren't universal across sticker paper brands.

## Recommended path

1. **Start with Tier 1** (~£670–800, including the cold roll laminator) — this is the genuine professional workflow (pigment ink + laminate) on current-generation equipment, not a compromise.
2. Sell for 3–6 months, hit your break-even point (~150–200 sales, fewer if you sell bundles).
3. Reinvest profits into **Tier 3 (UV DTF)** if volume justifies it — better long-term quality than Tier 2, and opens up new product categories.
