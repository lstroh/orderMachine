## 1. The two ad programmes — don't confuse them

Etsy has two completely separate advertising products, and mixing them up is the single most common cause of an unexpected bill.

### Etsy Ads (onsite) — optional, cost-per-click
- Promotes your listings within Etsy's own search results and pages.
- You set a daily budget yourself — **minimum ~£1/day**.
- Pay-per-click regardless of whether the click converts (typical CPC ~$0.20-$0.50).
- Fully optional and fully within your control — the closer analogue to eBay's Priority/CPC campaigns.

### Offsite Ads — conditional, cost-per-sale, and can become mandatory
- Etsy buys ads on your behalf on Google, Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest and other external platforms.
- **You only pay if a sale results from a click**, within a 30-day attribution window — so structurally it's cost-per-sale, like eBay's General Promoted Listings.
- **Fee: 15% of the total order amount** (item + shipping) if trailing 12-month shop revenue is under $10,000 USD (~£8,000); **12%** once at/above that threshold.
- **Below $10,000 trailing revenue: optional, can be switched off in Shop Manager → Settings → Offsite Ads.**
- **At/above $10,000 trailing revenue: mandatory for the lifetime of the shop** — cannot be opted out of again, even if revenue later drops back below the threshold.
- You have **no control** over which listings are promoted, which platforms are used, or budget — it's entirely Etsy's decision once enrolled.

**For this business at launch: opt out of Offsite Ads while eligible.** At £2-8/unit price points and modest early volume, £8,000 trailing revenue is a real way off, but it's worth checking this setting isn't left on by default when the shop opens — Etsy auto-enrols new shops and opting out is a manual step.

## 2. Full Etsy UK fee stack (so ad decisions aren't made in isolation)

| Fee | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Listing fee | ~£0.16 ($0.20 USD) per listing | Charged whether or not it sells; renews every 4 months, and again on every sale for multi-quantity listings |
| Transaction fee | 6.5% of item price + shipping + gift wrap | Applies to the shipping you charge too, not just the item |
| Payment processing | 4% + £0.20 per transaction | Etsy Payments is mandatory — no option to use your own processor |
| Regulatory Operating Fee | 0.32% of item price + shipping | UK-specific, since 2022 |
| Offsite Ads (if triggered) | 15% (or 12% once mandatory) | See above |
| Etsy Ads (if used) | Your chosen CPC budget | Fully optional, separate from the above |
| VAT on Etsy's fees | +20% on all the above | **Applies if not VAT-registered** — which applies to this business per the VAT-unregistered strategy in the Business Plan. Worth confirming this is factored into margin math, since it isn't a small addition. |

**Worked example at a representative £6 item + £1.20 shipping (£7.20 total), not VAT-registered:**
- Listing: £0.16
- Transaction (6.5%): £0.47
- Payment processing (4% + 20p): £0.49
- Regulatory (0.32%): £0.02
- Subtotal before VAT: £1.14
- **+20% VAT on fees (not VAT-registered): +£0.23**
- **Total fees without Offsite Ads: ≈£1.37 (≈19% of the sale)**
- If Offsite Ads applies (15%): add ≈£1.08 + VAT ≈£1.30 more → **total ≈£2.67 (≈37% of the sale)**

**This is meaningfully higher than eBay's ~13-27% depending on price point** (see the eBay guide), largely because of the fixed listing fee stacking with transaction + payment processing + the VAT-on-fees add-on that eBay doesn't currently charge in the same way. Worth factoring into channel comparison once Etsy is actually next in the sequence — Etsy isn't necessarily the cheaper channel it's sometimes assumed to be for a non-VAT-registered small shop.

## 3. What to actually spend on Etsy Ads (onsite)

- **A good return on ad spend (ROAS) benchmark on Etsy is commonly cited as 3x or higher** — i.e. £3 revenue for every £1 spent. Below 2x, most sellers are losing money once all the other Etsy fees are counted in.
- **Practical starting budget: £1-3/day**, enough to gather performance data without risking much.
- **A broader guideline for total onsite ad spend is roughly 5-10% of gross revenue** — comparable in spirit to eBay's independent-guidance range, though slightly higher given Etsy's CPC-not-CPS model carries more inherent risk.
- Same principle as eBay: **fix the free stuff first.** Etsy Ads amplify listings that already convert on their own; poor photos/titles/SEO won't be rescued by ad spend.

## 4. Recommended approach once Etsy is next in the launch sequence

1. **Confirm Offsite Ads is switched off** in Shop Manager settings at launch (it's on by default for new shops below the mandatory threshold).
2. **Start Etsy Ads (onsite) at £1-2/day**, once listings have decent photos/SEO in place — don't turn ads on before the free fundamentals are solid.
3. **Track ROAS from day one** — pause or reduce spend on anything sitting well below 3x after a couple of weeks of data.
4. **Revisit VAT-on-fees exposure** once real Etsy sales volume exists — it's a bigger drag than on eBay and worth knowing the actual number rather than the estimate above.
5. **Flag the $10,000 trailing-revenue Offsite Ads threshold** as a milestone to watch for, since crossing it locks in a mandatory 12-15% fee permanently — not something to trigger by accident while scaling.
